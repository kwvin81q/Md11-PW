Fan Trim Balance

Guided initial survey → trial weight → engine response → final mathematical balance estimate.

Engineering reference only — NOT an approved maintenance instruction. Verify effectivity, current AMM, applicable vibration limits, indexing, weight installation, and every calculation independently before acting. This tool cannot confirm engine condition or certify a balance.

First we will get our initial vibe

Record steady 1EL vibration and its phase angle from the EVM BALANCING screen during the acceleration portion of the survey. The AMM specifies 82% and 90% N1; use the same speed and pickup column for both runs.

Model selection determines how phase rotation is represented. Do not mix outputs from two monitor conventions.

AMM balance sensitivities are expressed in oz-in per mil. Other units can support relative trial-response calculations only if both runs use identical units.

Next we need to figure out this engine’s lead or lag

The initial estimate uses assumed response from previous engine balancing data. A measured trial is what reveals this engine's actual influence. Record the actual weight moment and tang used; they must match the trial run.

The AMM uses averaged phase lag and sensitivity from prior trim balance procedures to plan CW1. The illustrative AMM example uses 123° and 9.5 oz-in/mil; they are not universal settings and are not prefilled.

Record the actual CW1 trial

Weight options are copied from AMM p.40, Table 509; selection only populates the trial moment. Maximum 2 counterweights per tang. EVM fan-plane installed sum ≤20 oz-in, including any remaining trim weights. The app cannot verify actual hardware.

Manual references and limitations

MD-11 AMM Rev 99.0, 72-30-00-5-2, pp.30–46: EVM trim balance. pp.32–34: operating limits, signal conventions. pp.35–40: initial survey, CW1 selection, tang numbering and weight table. pp.41–43: CW1 vector difference and phase/sensitivity calculation. pp.44–46: repeated run, next correction and final survey. This is a single-speed mathematical estimate. The AMM requires consideration of 82% and 90% N1 and all relevant vibration observations, including whether the engine is eligible for this method.

Do not use if vibration is unstable, rotor damage/incorrect assembly is suspected, phase conventions are uncertain, existing TB weights have not been accounted for, or the current effectivity-approved maintenance data disagree. Manufacturer AMM and company procedures control.
